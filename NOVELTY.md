# What is new here, and what is not

This page compares Secure AI Foundations (SAF) v0.1 with public resources that cover similar ground. It was written so that users, reviewers and the author do not overstate what SAF adds. All pages were read on 2026-11-08. If you know a resource that should be here, please open an issue.

## Short answer

Most individual SAF controls are **not new**. Identity, least privilege, secrets, egress control, Kubernetes hardening, supply-chain integrity and logging are covered by NIST, CISA, OWASP and the Kubernetes community, and SAF cites them. What SAF tries to add is a **combination and a focus**:

1. **One deployer scenario:** connecting AI assistants, agents, retrieval and scoring models to an existing CRM or customer-data platform, including older, heavily integrated platforms.
2. **One first user group:** small and mid-size U.S. communications providers, with their specific data (CPNI and call detail information, 47 CFR Part 64 Subpart U) and their typical constraints (small teams, legacy platforms, vendor AI features switched on inside existing products).
3. **Three linked parts in one package:** a platform control baseline (T1), a governance template on the AI RMF functions (T2), and a platform readiness checklist (T3), cross-referenced to each other and mapped to CSF 2.0, SP 800-53 Rev. 5 and AI RMF 1.0 in one OLIR-style file.

We did not find a public resource that combines these three parts for this scenario. That finding comes from the comparison below and a small number of web searches. It is not an exhaustive survey.

## Where SAF is weaker than existing resources

- Fewer and shallower controls than OWASP AISVS.
- Governance coverage of the AI RMF is partial (no fairness, human-subject, workforce-diversity or environmental methods).
- No independent review yet; mappings are one person's judgment.
- No tested implementation results yet; v0.1 has not been piloted.

## How SAF intends to stay honest

- Cite the source of each practice rather than present it as new.
- When an authoritative NIST or CISA document covers the same point, defer to it and map to it.
- Record in `CHANGELOG.md` what feedback and pilots changed.
